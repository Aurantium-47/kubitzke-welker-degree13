# Primary sources and nearby questions

Links checked on 2026-11-04. The source papers are not bundled. These references identify the target, the classical ingredients, and the scope of nearby results. They are not an exhaustive priority review.

1. **Martina Kubitzke and Volkmar Welker, _Enumerative g-theorems for the Veronese construction for formal power series and graded algebras_.** [Authors' PDF, Question 4.4, p. 17](https://www.mathematik.uni-marburg.de/~welker/preprints/Veronese.pdf). This is the precise initial-difference simplicial f-vector problem addressed here.

2. **Francesco Brenti, _Some open problems on Coxeter groups and unimodality_ (2024).** [arXiv:2410.09897v1, Problems 2.9–2.10](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.09897v1). Problem 2.10 restates the target. The nearby numerical observation `d <= 9` and `h_i <= 100` belongs to Problem 2.9, the symmetric polytope h-vector problem; it is not an unrestricted-coefficient degree-nine theorem for Question 4.4.

3. **Jason Bell and Mark Skandera, _Multicomplexes and polynomials with real zeros_.** [Authors' PDF](https://www.lehigh.edu/~mas906/papers/logc.pdf). Question 1.1 / Conjecture 4.1 concerns the original coefficients, not their initial differences. Theorem 4.8 already gives fixed-degree finiteness for that coefficient problem. Section 2 records the binomial-operator characterizations of simplicial and multicomplex f-vectors.

4. **C. J. Smyth, _Totally positive algebraic integers of small trace_, Annales de l'Institut Fourier 34(3) (1984), 1–28.** [Publisher archive](https://numdam.org/item/AIF_1984__34_3_1_0/), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.5802/aif.975). The small-trace classification is classical prior art, not a new classification claimed here.

5. **Artūras Dubickas and Chris Smyth, _Two variations of a theorem of Kronecker_.** [Authors' PDF, Theorem K](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~chris/papers/variations1905-4.pdf). Records the classical unit-disc theorem used to derive the interval classification in the small-trace note. The extra PMC interval-formulation link in that note was blocked by a browser-check page during this packaging pass; it is not needed, since the argument follows from Theorem K and the displayed quadratic equation.

6. **Alper Ferudun, _Real-Rooted Integer Polynomials Satisfy a g-Theorem: Proof of a Conjecture of Brenti and Welker_ (17 October 2026, unrefereed preprint).** [Author's publication page](https://eulersolve.org/papers/owr-1782-017/). It asserts the weaker Macaulay conclusion and explicitly leaves the Kruskal–Katona strengthening open. Retaining the finite-degree Newton factor motivates this project's analytic estimate. The main degree-13 proof does **not** depend on the preprint's discrete Macaulay lemma. The alternative threshold in the general note is clearly separated.

7. **Mu and Welker, _On a question about real-rooted polynomials and f-polynomials of simplicial complexes_.** [arXiv:2503.24076v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.24076v2). Concerns the coefficient-vector problem, with partial sufficient conditions and approaches. It does not by itself settle the initial-difference target here.

8. **Feihu Liu, Ying Wang, and Zihao Zhang, _Counterexamples to the Mu–Welker recursive decomposition in every degree_.** [arXiv:2609.23694v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.23694v3). Disproves a recursive-real-rootedness proposal. Its counterexamples still have simplicial realizations, so that result is not a counterexample to either original f-vector target.

The degree-sensitive argument and exact degree-13 artifact are presented for inspection and reproduction, not as a certified first result. Targeted searches in the preceding review did not locate the same full-height degree-13 statement, but absence from such a search does not establish novelty.
